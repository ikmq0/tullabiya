# Batch 2: less pushed at the reader, a digital reader, a linked magazine, hard copies

Owner's brief (3 Nov 2026): the site distracts by pushing too much information at the reader; provide
a digital reader experience of the published magazine that is interactive, categorized and organized;
the magazine should be concluded and linked together; add a section for collecting the hard copy and
where to find it.

Colors do not change (owner, 3 Nov 2026). No color value, token or section background is edited.

Everything stays on the local simulation (`LocalApi`). No backend.

## Decisions

- **The home keeps all its sections, in order** (owner, 3 Nov 2026). What shrinks is what each section
  shows at rest: short title links instead of summaries and chips, detail behind a tap.
- **The digital reader is a choice.** It sits beside the existing site as another way to read. The nav,
  the story pages and the archive all keep working for someone who never opens it.
- **The site does not speak first.** No toasts, no numbers moving by themselves, no "since your last visit"
  line, no permanent notice bar. What the reader earned or missed is there when they go and look.
- **The edition rail stays on the home** (owner, 3 Nov 2026): the page remains long, and it helps the
  reader keep their place.
- **Sign-up opens only from its button** (owner, 3 Nov 2026). The first-read nudge from batch 1 is gone.
- **The reader is a PDF reader made for this magazine** (owner, 3 Nov 2026), at `/[lang]/read/[n]/`, with
  `/[lang]/read/` opening the current issue. It shows the printed pages, full screen, with its own controls
  and without the site's bar or footer. Web story pages are a separate way to read, not part of it.
- **Pages are vector, not pictures** (owner, 3 Nov 2026: JPEG pages looked scanned). Each page is an SVG
  generated from the PDF with text as paths and photos resampled inside it, shown as a plain `<img>`, so
  text stays sharp at every zoom. `pNN.jpg` remains for thumbnails and as the fallback.
- **The PDFs' layout is the reader's data.** They hold word and image positions but no outline, and their
  extracted text is damaged. `scripts/extract_layout.py` keeps positions only; extracted text is never
  shown to readers or searched.
- **On a laptop the tools sit at the side, by language** (owner, 3 Nov 2026): the right in Arabic, the left
  in English, with panels opening beside them. Contents is a list docked on that side, open by default,
  like a PDF reader's outline. Trackpad pinch and Ctrl+wheel zoom about the cursor, and the
  mouse can pan. Phones keep the bottom bar.
- **Contents go to the place in the file** (owner, 3 Nov 2026). Tapping a section or story in the reader's
  Contents turns to its page and zooms in on it, from `pdfRegion` in the story data. Reading an article in
  the reader never leaves for a separate page. The one exception is a story with no printed page. A jump
  ends as sharp as a manual zoom, lands exactly on the story, breaks nothing, and never locks the reader in:
  they can pan, zoom, jump again or turn the page at once, and any input interrupts the motion.
- **Linked in both directions.** A story links to its printed page in the reader (`pdfPage`), and a printed
  page lists the stories on it. Stories lead to the next one in the issue's `storyIds` order. Issues lead to the
  previous and next issue by number.
- **"Concluded" means the issue ends.** The last story and the last page lead to one end-of-issue screen:
  what the issue held by section, what the reader finished, the next and previous issue, and the
  hard copy. This serves the anti-goal in the design spec: the issue is finite and says so.
- **Position is remembered.** Last view and page per issue go through the `Api` seam, with a contract test.
- **The phone is the reader's real home.** Controls sit within thumb reach at the bottom, safe areas are
  respected, nothing depends on hover. A desktop layout is an adaptation of the phone one, not the reverse.
- **Structured sections.** The issue reads as sections, then stories. A contents sheet is always one tap
  away, showing where the reader is. Printed pages are grouped by section where stories say which page
  they are on; back issues without stories are plain page sequences.
- **Movement follows the finger.** Pages track the drag and settle by velocity; pinch zooms around the
  fingers, double tap zooms, and a zoomed page pans within its edges. Transform and opacity only, no
  page-curl effects, reduced-motion respected. A sharper page image loads only when the reader zooms.
- **Photos on the page open, and keep their quality** (owner, 3 Nov 2026). Tapping a photo on a printed page
  grows it from its place into a full-screen view, with zoom and swiping between that page's photos. Areas
  come from the PDF's image positions. The viewer shows the PDF's original embedded image, unchanged and
  undistorted, over a semi-transparent background that keeps the page dimly in view. It opens at a size the
  photo can support, and its zoom limit comes from its real pixels. Photos inside a zoomed page swap to the
  same originals, so they stay as sharp as the text allows.
  Larger originals from the editors, placed in `public/archive/<n>/photos/`, raise that limit by themselves.
- **Highlights and comments belong to the reader.** An explicit highlight mode marks text on the page,
  snapping to the PDF's word positions, so marking never fights with panning. Marks go through the `Api`
  seam, stay on the device, are never shown to anyone else, and are listed in My Corner by a crop of the
  page. A reader may send a comment to the editors, which uses the existing
  `submitUserContent('comment', ...)` and stays `pending`, as every user submission is pre-moderated.
  Public comments wait for a real moderation backend.
- **Hard copies are a section, not an order form.** Copies are collected from **Building 68 at KFUPM**,
  anytime (owner, 3 Nov 2026). `src/data/pickup.json` holds that one point; the list shape allows more
  later. Room and a map link are not known: optional fields, left out rather than invented, and
  `docs/handoff.md` lists them as open. It has a page, `/[lang]/hard-copy/`, plus links from the reader's
  end screen and the footer. No reservations: nothing stands behind them yet.

## Not changing

- Colors, tokens, fonts, the slanted FAST shapes.
- What the hunt, polls and stamps do once a reader opens them.
- No OCR. Stories exist only for issue 21; other issues are linked by page and by issue.
